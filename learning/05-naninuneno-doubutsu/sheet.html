Row 5 of the gojuon — な に ぬ ね の — through animals: a catfish and a sloth, a chicken and a monkey, a cat and a mouse, a field hare and a stray cat. Say the animal, then watch it do something. The animals variation — な行's toughest cut, since ぬ has no animal at all.

Kids / city pop · playful (house voice)~92 BPMComprehensible inputYouTube ~3:00 · vertical ~1:00
01

The idea

Words alone blend (findings F-10), so every animal is taught in a small action — なまけもの が ねる, のうさぎ が はねる — and it recombines with earlier rows (くま, かえる, はっぱ, とり + verbs ねる・はしる・はねる・いる return), keeping the ladder (F-13).

The five-kana row な に ぬ ね の closes every verse as the sung-together refrain (F-07). The 3-minute cut adds a field-friends bridge and a sentences-only review, not a loop (F-12).

This is the animals variation — ぬ is the single hardest kana in the gojuon and has no beginner animal at all, so its verse leans on a stuffed animal (ぬいぐるみ), a pond frog, and a soft cloth (a pet's blanket). な and に also reach a little (a slug, a macaque, a python). The cleaner な-row cuts are home and nature. Pairs with the kids voice.
