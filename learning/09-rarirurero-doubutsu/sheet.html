Row 9 of the gojuon — ら り る れ ろ — through animals: a camel and a lion and a sea otter, a squirrel and a tortoise, a red panda and an antelope, a donkey. Say the animal, then watch it do something. The animals variation of three ら-row songs — the toughest yet at る and ろ.

01

The idea

Words alone blend (findings F-10), so every animal is taught in a small action — らくだ が あるく, れいよう が はしる — recombining earlier rows (ねこ, いぬ, おそい + verbs あるく・いる・とぶ return; F-13). Row closes every verse (F-07); the longer cut adds a bridge + review (F-12).

The five-kana row ら り る れ ろ is the sung-together refrain. ら/り/れ carry strong animals (camel, lion, otter, squirrel, tortoise, red panda, antelope).

Honest note: る has NO beginner animal (its verse borrows a blue azure-bird, its colour, and a magnifier), and ろ has only ろば (donkey), so its verse adds a cat in an alley and a dog in a corridor. Two documented detours, like か行's け or な行's ぬ. The things and colours cuts are the cleaner ら-row songs. Pairs with the kids voice.
