Row 2 of the gojuon — か き く け こ — taught through animals a child already loves: a crab, a fox, a whale, a caterpillar, a puppy. Say the animal, then watch it do something. The first of three か-row songs (animals · town · nature) so the same five kana get practiced without a song ever repeating itself.

City pop · female (house voice)~92 BPMComprehensible inputYouTube ~3:00 · vertical ~1:00
01

The idea

Words alone blend and don't teach production (findings F-10), so every target animal is shown in a tiny action — かに が あるく, きんぎょ が およぐ — and the row recombines with row 1 (うみ, いぬ, さんぽ and the verbs いく・いる・みる come back), so か行 also practices あ行.

The five-kana row か き く け こ closes every verse as the sung-together refrain, where Suno's unison habit recruits the singer (F-07). The 3-minute cut reaches length with a zoo-day bridge and a sentences-only review, not a loop (F-12).

This is the animals variation. か行 gets two more songs — まち (a town day) and しぜん (nature) — teaching the identical five kana with fresh vocabulary, so a learner can practice reading the row several times over without the input ever going stale. Honest note: か行 has no beginner け animal, so the け verse is a small 'kitten's morning' detour (けさ・けむし・けいと).
