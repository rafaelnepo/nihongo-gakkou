五十音 — STYLE PROMPTS
Nepô's idea: restyle the traditional chants. Four directions, most-recommended
first. All four keep the same lyric file — only the style prompt changes, which
is exactly what makes this a good series slot.

--- A · BOOM-BAP HIP HOP  ★ the non-obvious one, and the best fit ---
五十音 IS a flow exercise. Rappers drill articulation with tongue twisters for
the same reason actors do, and a consonant row is alliteration practice by
construction. The twister lines want to be rapped; the kana run wants to be the
hook. This is the version that could actually get listened to for pleasure.
boom bap hip hop, 88 BPM, clear-voiced rapper on the verses
with a full group chanting the hook in unison,
dusty upright bass, crisp snare, minimal piano loop,
dry forward vocal, no reverb on the lead, no autotune,
extremely clear consonants, hard rhythmic diction,
space between the verse and the chant, no ad-libs

--- B · MATSURI ONDO — the traditional shape ---
This is how a chant like this actually gets drilled: one voice calls, everybody
answers. Hand claps on the kana. Comes with dance steps.
Japanese matsuri ondo, 92 BPM,
one lead singer calling and a large group answering in unison,
taiko drum, shinobue flute, shamisen, hand claps,
festive and driving but unhurried, dry vocals,
extremely clear enunciation, big gap before each group answer, no ad-libs

--- C · GOSPEL CHOIR — makes the kana run enormous ---
Leader and congregation. The unison answer becomes the emotional payoff rather
than a drill, which is a real argument for it: nobody resents singing this one.
gospel call and response, 84 BPM,
solo lead vocal answered by a full choir in unison,
Hammond organ, tambourine, hand claps, upright piano,
joyful and warm, dry lead vocal, very clear enunciation,
big choir answers, no ad-libs

--- D · BOSSA NOVA — the one with a real cost ---
Warm, adult, and personal to this project. But bossa's syncopation pulls
AGAINST the mora grid, and mora-timing is half of what this song teaches.
Generate it for pleasure, not for the drill.
bossa nova, 96 BPM, soft male vocal with a group answering,
nylon string guitar, brushed drums, light upright bass, subtle flute,
relaxed and warm, dry vocal, very clear enunciation, no ad-libs
