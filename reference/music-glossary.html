Sound Words

音楽のことば

A plain-language map of how a song is built and the knobs you can turn — so you can name exactly what you are hearing, finding, or missing. Skip to §4 when a take feels off and you want the word for it.

𝄆
01

The parts of a song

The building blocks. A song is these blocks put in an order — that order is the structure (§2).

a common pop shape Intro→ Verse→ Chorus→ Verse→ Chorus→ Bridge→ Chorus→ Outro
Intro

The opening. Sets tempo and mood before the singing. Ours is the spoken/​sung cold open.

Verse

The storytelling section. New words each time, usually the same music. Where our lesson content lives.

Pre-chorus

A short ramp that builds tension and hands you into the chorus.

Chorus hook

The repeating high point — same words and music each time. The bit you remember. Our あいうえお refrain plays this role.

Hook

The single stickiest phrase — often the chorus, but any line designed to lodge in your head.

Refrain

A recurring line, shorter than a full chorus, often closing each verse.

Bridge

A contrasting section, usually once, late — new melody or feel to break the pattern before the final chorus. Our v3 "day" bridge.

Outro

The ending — a wind-down, a repeat of the hook, or a tag.

Tag

A tiny repeated phrase at the very end to button the song shut.

Breakdown

Strip most parts away, leave one or two — makes space, then rebuild.

Drop

The release after a build — the moment the full arrangement/beat lands.

Vamp

A short chord/rhythm figure repeated to hold a groove (often under ad-libs).

02

Common shapes

How the blocks get ordered. Naming the shape tells everyone what to expect.

Verse–Chorus (most pop) V→C→ V→C→ Bridge→C
Strophic — same music, new words (our lesson verses) V1→V2→ V3→V4→V5
AABA — 32-bar / standards A→A→ B→A
Through-composed — no repeats (五十音) A→B→ C→D→…

Our lesson songs are strophic verses + a returning refrain.

The v3 long cut adds a bridge (the day) and a review pass so it can run 3 minutes without looping — that's structure doing the work, not repetition.

03

The variables — the knobs you can turn

Change one at a time and you can hear what it did. Each knob below has two poles you can push toward.

Time — how it moves

Tempo BPM
Speed, in beats per minute. slow ↔ fast. Ours sits ~92 for singability.
Meter time signature
Beats per bar — 4/4 (march/pop), 3/4 (waltz/lullaby sway), 6/8 (rolling).
Groove feel
How the beat leans. straight ↔ swung; tight ↔ laid-back.
Syncopation
Accents landing off the main beat — more = funkier/less predictable.
Density note rate
How many notes/words per second. sparse ↔ busy. Busy blurs diction.

Pitch — the notes

Key
The home note/chord family the song centres on.
Scale mode
major (bright) ↔ minor (moody). The quickest mood switch there is.
Register
How high or low. low (calm) ↔ high (bright/urgent).
Melody
The tune — the line you'd hum.
Harmony
Notes stacked under the melody. Two singers a third apart = harmony.
Chord progression
The order the chords move in — the emotional "road" beneath everything.

Sound — the colour

Timbre tone
The character of a sound regardless of pitch — warm, bright, dusty, glassy.
Instrumentation
Which instruments play. Our lever for genre (koto vs Rhodes vs guitar).
Texture density
How many layers at once. thin/bare ↔ thick/lush.
Arrangement
Who plays what, when — the plan of parts across the song. The biggest lever after melody.

Voice — how it's sung

Lead
The main vocal carrying the melody/words.
Backing BGV
Support vocals — harmonies, echoes, "oohs." A texture, not a second lead.
Unison
Two+ voices on the same note/words. Where a listener joins in — our F-07 refrain.
Call & response
A lead sings, a group answers. Good pedagogy; Suno handles the group as texture.
Diction enunciation
How clearly words are pronounced. The whole product of a teaching song.
Phrasing
Where the singer breathes and stresses — the "punctuation" of a melody.
Ad-lib
Improvised extras (runs, "yeah"s). We exclude these — they blur a lesson.
Melisma
One syllable stretched over many notes. Beautiful, but it hides mora timing.
Vibrato
A gentle wobble in pitch held on a note.
Falsetto / head vs chest
Light airy high voice vs. full grounded voice.

Space & energy — the mix and the arc

Dynamics
Loud vs soft, and the change between them. Flat dynamics = dull.
Reverb wet/dry
Sense of room/space. dry (close, clear) ↔ wet (distant, washy). Wet blurs words.
Delay echo
Repeats of a sound — rhythmic space.
Mix
The balance of everything. "Vocal is buried" = a mix note.
EQ tone balance
Bright vs dark; thin vs boomy; "muddy" = too much low-mid.
Build / riser
Rising energy toward a drop or chorus.
Tension & release
The core of feeling in music — set up an expectation, then pay it off.
Contrast / variation
Making a section differ from its neighbour. The cure for "boring."
04

Saying what you mean

You already hear these. This is the word for each, and the knob it points at — so a note lands precisely instead of "something's off."

What you feelWhat to sayThe knob
The words blur together"Diction isn't clear — more space between phrases, less reverb."diction · density · reverb
It drags / sleepy"Lift the energy — it may be dynamics or arrangement, not tempo."energy · dynamics
Too fast to follow"Drop the tempo a little; it's outrunning the lesson."tempo
Gets boring after a bit"Needs contrast — a bridge, or change the texture in round 2."arrangement · variation
Too repetitive"Vary one thing each pass — don't loop the same block."variation
Voice is buried"Vocal needs to sit on top — more presence, less wash behind it."mix · reverb
Feels childish"Warmer timbre, calmer groove — less bright and bouncy."timbre · feel
Feels cold / robotic"More human phrasing and dynamics; a touch of warmth."phrasing · dynamics
The ending just stops"Give it an outro or a tag to land on."structure
Muddy / cluttered"Thin the texture; clean up the low-mids."texture · EQ
I can't tell the words apart in the list"Same-onset words blur — put them in sentences." (our F-10)density · diction
It didn't feel like a conversation"Suno makes a duet trade sections, not talk — need texture or two takes." (F-07)arrangement
05

How this maps to what we've learned

The vocabulary above, tied to the project's own findings — so a term and a finding point at the same thing.

Unison → F-07

Suno sings a verse's last line in unison. Put the phrase everyone should sing there.

Density/diction → F-10

A list of same-onset words blends. Sentences add space and context — clarity and pedagogy at once.

Structure → F-12

Suno performs pasted lyrics once, so length = lyric quantity. Reach 3 min by adding a bridge + review, not a loop.

Timbre/feel → the voice

City-pop female is the house voice for basic songs; lo-fi and koto are kept for moodier content.

Arrangement → F-04

A part change needs a ~2-line block — finer than that and Suno holds one voice or turns it into a reading.

Groove → the mora grid

Bossa/​swing pulls against even mora timing; straight grooves protect the rhythm the song teaches.
